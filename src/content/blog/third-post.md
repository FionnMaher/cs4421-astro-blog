**Pac-Man** began in Japan as an arcade game developed by Namco and released there in 1980. The designer **Toru Iwatani** wanted to create a game with a playful character and broad appeal, rather than another space-themed shooter. The result put players in a maze, collecting dots while avoiding four pursuing ghosts.

In Japan, the game was called **Puck Man**, a name inspired by the character’s shape. When the game was prepared for release in North America by Midway, its name was changed to Pac-Man. The new name also avoided the possibility of altering the initial letter of “Puck” on arcade cabinets.

The maze and its ghosts gave Pac-Man a different feel from many games of the period. Players could learn the layout and use corners, tunnels, and brief power-ups to plan a route. Eating a power pellet temporarily let Pac-Man turn the tables and chase the ghosts. The four ghosts also had distinct movement behaviors, making them feel less like identical obstacles.

Pac-Man became an international hit in arcades, and its reach quickly extended beyond the original cabinet. Home versions brought it to consoles and computers; sequels and spin-offs expanded the series. The character also appeared in merchandise, animation, and other media, making Pac-Man one of gaming’s most recognizable figures.

More than four decades later, the original game is still easy to pick up. Its success came from combining approachable controls with a maze that rewards attention and route planning. Pac-Man helped show that arcade games could feature memorable characters and appeal to a broad audience—not just players looking for a space battle.
