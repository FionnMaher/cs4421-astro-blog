Video games grew from small experiments in computing into one of the world's most popular forms of entertainment. Their history is a story of changing technology, inventive designers, and players finding new ways to play together.

## Early experiments

In the 1950s and 1960s, researchers used large computers to create simple interactive demonstrations. In 1958, physicist **William Higinbotham** created *Tennis for Two*, an oscilloscope-based game that let two people play a simple tennis match. In 1962, a team at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology developed *Spacewar!*, a computer game that spread among programmers and universities.

These projects were not commercial products, but they showed that computers could be used for play as well as calculation. As computers became more accessible, the idea of interactive electronic entertainment moved closer to the public.

## Arcades and the home console

The early 1970s brought video games into public spaces. *Computer Space* appeared in 1971, and Atari's *Pong* became a major arcade hit the following year. Arcades filled with cabinets offering quick, competitive games, while companies began experimenting with systems people could connect to home televisions.

The **Magnavox Odyssey**, released in 1972, was the first home video game console. Dedicated home systems became more common later in the decade, and cartridges eventually made it possible to play different games on the same machine. The arcade and home markets grew together, each encouraging new kinds of games and hardware.

## The rise of familiar icons

During the late 1970s and 1980s, arcade games reached a wider audience and introduced memorable characters and worlds. *Space Invaders*, *Pac-Man*, and *Donkey Kong* became international successes. At home, the Nintendo Entertainment System helped revive and expand the console market in many regions, while personal computers made games available to an increasing number of households.

The technology kept progressing. More powerful hardware brought richer sound, color, scrolling environments, and longer adventures. In the 1990s, 3D graphics became a defining feature of many consoles and computer games, changing how players explored virtual spaces.

## Games connect the world

The internet and faster home connections changed how games were played. Multiplayer experiences expanded beyond the living room, and digital stores made it easier to discover and download games. Smartphones later put simple, portable games in the hands of billions of people.

Today, games range from independent projects made by small teams to vast online worlds built by large studios. They are played on consoles, computers, phones, and handheld devices, and they bring people together to compete, collaborate, and tell stories.

The hardware and styles will continue to change, but the central idea remains familiar: games invite us to take part. From an oscilloscope tennis match to a shared online adventure, each generation has found new ways to turn technology into play.
