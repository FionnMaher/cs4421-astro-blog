Minecraft began as an independent project by Swedish game developer **Markus Persson**, known as Notch. An early version was made available to players in 2009, inviting them to explore a world made of blocks, gather materials, and build their own creations. Its open-ended design made player imagination central to the experience.

The game developed in public, with feedback from its growing community helping shape new features. Players could mine resources, craft tools, construct shelters, and explore procedurally generated worlds. Survival and creative play offered different ways to approach the same flexible sandbox.

In 2011, Minecraft reached its official 1.0 release. By then, its mix of exploration, crafting, building, and multiplayer had already attracted a large audience. The simple visual style also made the game recognizable while allowing its worlds to grow in scale and complexity.

Microsoft acquired Mojang, the studio behind Minecraft, in 2014. Over time, the game expanded across computers, consoles, and mobile devices. Different editions brought the experience to more platforms, while regular updates and community-made creations continued to add new ways to play.

Minecraft's history is closely tied to the people who play it. From elaborate buildings and adventure maps to collaborative servers and classroom projects, players have used its block-based tools to make the game their own. Its lasting appeal comes from a simple invitation: enter a world, gather what you need, and decide what to create.
