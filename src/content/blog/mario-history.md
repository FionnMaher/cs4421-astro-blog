Mario first appeared in **Donkey Kong**, an arcade game released by Nintendo in 1981. He was originally called Jumpman, a carpenter who had to climb platforms and avoid obstacles to rescue a character from a giant ape. The game introduced the jumping action that would become closely associated with Mario.

The character returned in **Mario Bros.** in 1983, an arcade game that introduced his brother Luigi and focused on clearing enemies from pipes. Two years later, **Super Mario Bros.** arrived on the Nintendo Entertainment System. Its side-scrolling worlds, secrets, power-ups, and responsive controls helped establish a lasting template for console platform games.

As Nintendo's hardware changed, Mario's adventures changed with it. **Super Mario 64**, released in 1996, brought the series into three-dimensional environments and gave players more freedom to explore. Later games experimented with new settings, movement, and multiplayer, while returning to the series' familiar emphasis on discovery and precise play.

Mario also became the center of games beyond platforming, including kart racing, sports, and party games. These spin-offs introduced the character to players who enjoyed different kinds of competition and play.

More than four decades after his arcade debut, Mario remains one of gaming's most recognizable characters. His history reflects the evolution of video games themselves, from compact arcade challenges to expansive worlds designed for home consoles and many kinds of players.
