Snake's basic idea is instantly recognizable: guide a growing line around a screen, collect objects, and avoid running into yourself or the walls. That simple formula has roots in the early arcade era.

One important early example was **Blockade**, released by Gremlin in 1976. It was a two-player arcade game in which each player steered a line that left a trail behind it. The goal was to outlast the opponent without crashing. Blockade helped establish the trail-avoidance style that later games would adapt in many forms.

Over the following years, versions of the idea appeared on arcade machines, home computers, and handheld devices. The rules were easy to understand, but surviving became harder as the trail lengthened and the available space shrank. This made Snake well suited to short rounds and quick rematches.

Snake reached an especially broad audience in 1997, when Nokia included a version on its **6110** mobile phone. Nokia engineer **Taneli Armanto** created the game for the phone. Its monochrome display and simple controls were a natural fit: players steered the snake with the keypad and tried to beat their previous score. As Nokia phones spread around the world, Snake became a familiar part of everyday mobile life.

Later phones and other platforms introduced colorful graphics, new modes, and multiplayer variations, but the core challenge stayed much the same. Snake's history shows how a clear idea can travel from arcades to pocket-sized screens—and remain fun even when the technology is modest.
